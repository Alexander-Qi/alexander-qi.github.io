My research program focuses on the quantitative mechanistic analysis of transient phenomena within complex chemical systems, bridging custom spectroscopic instrumentation and physical organic chemistry. By integrating self-developed spectroscopic platforms with high-level computational analysis, I aim to resolve fundamental ambiguities in radical-mediated and photochemical processes. This work begins with the development of advanced tools, specifically the U-PSD TREPR spectrometer and its associated automated data-processing frameworks, which provide the necessary temporal resolution to capture elusive intermediates.

Building upon this instrumental foundation, my work focuses on the quantitative reassessment of catalytic cycles and reactivity scales—from the direct observation of complete photocatalytic cycles to the systematic revision of α-aminoalkyl radical halogen atom transfer kinetics—as well as the rational design and mechanistic dissection of modular organic photocatalysts such as _N_-boryl pyridyl scaffolds. More recently, I have extended this spectroscopic and kinetic framework into the ultrafast regime, combining femtosecond-to-nanosecond transient absorption, hard-constrained global kinetic modeling, and excited-state electronic-structure calculations to investigate visible-light-driven bond homolysis and the excited-state dynamics of free hydrogen atom generation. Across these projects, I strive to transform qualitative chemical intuition into a quantitative, falsifiable mechanistic discipline.
